How it works

Fuel & Burn answers one question: are you eating less or more than you burn, by how much, and where is your body heading? Everything else supports that.

Getting started

  • Log what you eat on most days. Rough is fine. A day that's half logged helps less than you'd think, so it's worth adding the snacks too.
  • Weigh in on a few mornings a week, after the toilet and before eating or drinking.
  • After about a week, the app starts learning what you really burn. The more weeks you log, the better your numbers fit you.

Why the week, not the day

Your body doesn't settle up every evening. A bigger day followed by ordinary ones averages out, and that average is what moves your weight. So Today shows your average per day over the week (or the past 30 days, or the year). A bigger day is never a reason to eat less the next day: just eat as usual.

Reading the bar on Today

Orange fill
What you ate on average per day in the period. Today itself joins the average once the day is over.
Green zone
Your goal range: how much to eat for the goal and pace you chose.
Blue tick
Your maintenance, when it isn't the end of the bar: what you'd eat to stay the same.
The line under it
The difference from maintenance, and where it sits: On target, A bit under your goal, Lighter deficit than planned, or More than planned when you're eating a lot less than your plan. That last one is a nudge to eat more: a deficit bigger than planned costs muscle and energy.

Your weight trend

One weigh-in can move by a kilo with water, salt, food in your stomach or your cycle. The trend line is the average of the past seven days, so those swings even out. A reading that's far from the ones around it is shown but left out of the trend. Watch the line over weeks, not the number on a single morning.

Your maintenance

Maintenance is how much you burn on a usual day. It starts as an estimate from your sex, age, height, weight and activity level (the Mifflin–St Jeor formula, or Katch–McArdle once you log body fat twice). Formulas are often off by around 10% for a given person, so that's only the start.

Then it learns. For each full week with food on most days and weigh-ins around it, the app compares what you ate with how your weight trend moved. If you ate about the same and your trend went down, you burned more than the formula thought. Each such week moves the estimate towards your own number. On Burn, the dots show how many of the last four weeks it learned from:

  • Estimate from your profile: the formula only, for now.
  • Learning from your data: your weeks blended with the formula.
  • Tuned to you: mostly your own data, and it keeps following it.

You'll always see a range, never a single exact number, because no app can know your burn to the calorie. Days with very little logged are treated as incomplete and skipped, so a forgotten meal never makes your plan smaller. A week that looks very different from the others, say a holiday or a stomach bug, is left out.

Goals and paces

Lose fat is a deficit of 10–15% of maintenance (Gentle) or 15–20% (Steady). Maintain is within 5%. Build muscle is a surplus of 5–10% or 10–15%, which works with strength training. There's no faster pace on purpose: bigger deficits cost muscle and are hard to keep up. Your plan never goes below your estimated resting burn.

Activity

Your usual activity is already part of your maintenance. So when you log a walk, a run or a workout, only the difference from a typical day changes your balance, not the whole thing. That stops the same steps being counted twice. Activity numbers are rough, around ±20%, and shown as “~215”.

Fuel: protein, fat, carbs and fiber

The bars show each against your targets. Going past a target is information, not a mistake: it shows as “+12 g extra”. Protein matters most while losing fat, since it helps keep muscle. You can change the targets with “Edit targets”; the sheet tells you if they don't add up to your calorie plan.

Recognising food

Type what you ate in any words, say it with “Speak”, or take a photo. AI turns that into foods and amounts; the calories then come from the ANSES-CIQUAL food table, not from the AI's guess. Check the amounts before adding: you can change each one, and a dish stays one entry under your own name for it. Without a connection you can still search the food list, use My foods and Quick add.

Expected vs actual

In Trends, the app compares the change your food diary predicts with what your weight trend did over the same weeks. Close means the estimate fits you. If your weight came out higher, water, salt and the odd unlogged snack are the usual reasons, and the estimate adjusts by itself. It's never a reason to eat less.

Calm view

If numbers make you anxious, turn on “Calm view” in the ⋮ menu. It hides calories and grams and shows words and shapes instead: “room for more”, “on target”, “trending down”. Everything else works the same.

Your data

Everything you log stays on this phone, with no account. That also means it's gone if the phone is lost or the browser's data is cleared, so use “Export backup” now and then and keep the file somewhere safe; “Restore from backup” brings it back. “Delete all my data” erases everything. More in the privacy policy.

Not medical advice

Fuel & Burn is a wellness app and its numbers are estimates. If you have a health condition or are pregnant, talk to a doctor before changing how you eat. If food or your weight starts to feel like a struggle, please reach out to a doctor or an eating-disorder support service near you. You can take a break from logging at any time.
